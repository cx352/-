# Bulking Decision Engine

## 1. Purpose

This module determines how to adjust nutrition and training during a natural bodybuilding muscle-gain phase.

The system prioritizes:

1. Long-term muscle gain
2. Controlled fat gain
3. Training performance
4. Recovery
5. Sustainable nutrition
6. Individual response data

Do not make major changes based on a single day's bodyweight.

---

# 2. Required Input Data

The system should collect:

### Bodyweight
- Daily morning bodyweight
- 7-day average
- 14-day average
- 28-day average

### Body composition proxies
- Waist circumference
- Progress photos
- Subjective muscularity
- Optional body-fat estimate

### Training
- Main lift performance
- Repetitions
- Load
- RIR/RPE
- Total productive sets
- Training frequency

### Recovery
- Sleep duration
- Sleep quality
- Muscle soreness
- Joint fatigue
- General fatigue
- Motivation

### Activity
- Daily steps
- Cardio
- Major changes in daily activity

### Nutrition
- Calories
- Protein
- Carbohydrates
- Fat
- Meal consistency

---

# 3. Primary Decision Principle

The system should NEVER use bodyweight alone.

Decision hierarchy:

1. Bodyweight trend
2. Waist trend
3. Training performance
4. Recovery
5. Visual changes
6. Hunger
7. Step/activity changes

Use multiple indicators before making a meaningful calorie adjustment.

---

# 4. Target Rate of Weight Gain

For a natural bodybuilding hypertrophy phase, use a conservative rate of gain.

### General starting target

Approximately:

- 0.10–0.25% bodyweight/week

For a 78 kg athlete:

- approximately +0.08 to +0.20 kg/week

This is a starting range, not a rigid requirement.

The appropriate rate depends on:

- training age
- current body fat
- recent dieting history
- rate of previous weight gain
- training performance
- recovery
- individual response

---

# 5. Initial Calorie Strategy

Do not automatically start with a large calorie surplus.

Start close to estimated maintenance.

Then use observed weight trends to determine the required adjustment.

Recommended initial approach:

- maintenance
- or a small surplus of approximately 100–200 kcal/day

For an athlete coming out of a contest-prep phase, a maintenance or small-surplus period may be preferable to immediately using a large surplus.

---

# 6. Decision Window

Use:

### Minimum
7 days

### Preferred
14 days

### Confirmation
28 days

Do not make large changes because of:

- one heavy meal
- one low weigh-in
- one high weigh-in
- one unusually good workout
- one unusually bad workout

---

# 7. Decision Matrix

## Situation A — Weight stable + performance improving

Indicators:

- 7–14 day weight trend stable
- waist stable
- strength/repetitions improving
- recovery good

Action:

→ Maintain calories.

Reason:

Bodyweight stability does not necessarily mean no muscle gain.

Possible recomposition can occur, especially in:

- relatively trained but recently dieting athletes
- athletes returning from a contest
- athletes with sufficient protein
- athletes whose training quality is improving

---

# 8. Situation B — Weight slowly increasing + performance improving

Indicators:

- weight increasing approximately within target range
- waist increasing slowly
- strength improving
- recovery good

Action:

→ Maintain calories.

Do NOT automatically increase calories every week.

Reason:

The current intake is already producing the desired response.

---

# 9. Situation C — Weight increasing too quickly + waist increasing rapidly

Indicators:

- weight gain exceeds target for multiple weeks
- waist increases noticeably
- visual condition deteriorates quickly

Action:

Reduce energy intake slightly.

Initial adjustment:

- approximately -100 to -200 kcal/day

Prefer adjusting carbohydrates and/or dietary fats while keeping:

- protein sufficient
- training quality high

Reassess for 10–14 days.

---

# 10. Situation D — Weight stable/down + performance declining

Indicators:

- 7–14 day weight trend flat or decreasing
- repeated performance decline
- poor recovery
- increased hunger
- reduced training quality

Action:

Increase energy intake.

Initial adjustment:

+100 to +200 kcal/day

Prefer carbohydrates around training.

Reassess for 10–14 days.

---

# 11. Situation E — Weight decreasing + performance stable

Possible interpretations:

- actual calorie deficit
- reduced glycogen
- reduced gut content
- increased activity
- temporary water fluctuation

Action:

Do NOT immediately increase calories.

Check:

- 14-day weight trend
- waist
- training performance
- sleep
- steps
- hunger

If the downward trend persists and muscle-gain performance is compromised:

→ increase calories modestly.

---

# 12. Situation F — Weight increasing but performance not improving

Potential explanations:

- excessive calorie surplus
- insufficient training stimulus
- excessive fatigue
- poor exercise selection
- poor sleep
- inaccurate calorie tracking
- water/glycogen changes

Action:

Do NOT automatically add calories.

First investigate:

1. Training quality
2. RIR/RPE
3. Weekly volume
4. Recovery
5. Sleep
6. Steps
7. Nutrition accuracy

If bodyweight and waist are both increasing rapidly:

→ reduce calories slightly.

---

# 13. Situation G — Weight stable + waist increasing + performance unchanged

This pattern suggests that additional weight may not be producing a proportional training benefit.

Action:

- Maintain or slightly reduce calories.
- Review training quality.
- Review daily activity.
- Review food-tracking accuracy.
- Continue monitoring for 10–14 days.

Do not assume that every kilogram gained is muscle.

---

# 14. Situation H — Weight stable + waist stable + performance stable

Action:

→ Maintain current intake.

This is not automatically a problem.

Possible explanations:

- maintenance intake
- body recomposition
- insufficient stimulus
- insufficient recovery
- measurement noise

Investigate before changing calories.

---

# 15. Carbohydrate Adjustment Rules

Carbohydrates are the preferred first adjustment when:

- training performance is declining
- recovery is poor
- bodyweight is falling
- activity is high
- hunger is increasing
- the athlete is already consuming adequate protein and fat

Typical adjustment:

+20–40 g carbohydrate/day

or

-20–40 g carbohydrate/day

Then monitor for 7–14 days.

Do not repeatedly change carbohydrates every day.

---

# 16. Protein

Protein should remain relatively stable.

General hypertrophy range:

Approximately:

1.6–2.2 g/kg/day

Higher intake is not automatically better.

When calories are adjusted, prioritize changing:

- carbohydrate
-膳食脂肪

在对蛋白质进行不必要的大幅调整之前。

---

# 17. 脂肪

保持充足的膳食脂肪摄入。

避免仅仅为了增加碳水化合物而过度减少脂肪。

需考虑：

- 激素健康
- 饮食依从性
- 食物偏好
- 餐次满意度
