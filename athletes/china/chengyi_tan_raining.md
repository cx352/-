Chengyi Tan — Training System

1. Training System Overview

Current public training framework

Chengyi Tan has publicly presented a Push / Pull / Legs training framework together with Kai Sheng Wang.

The system emphasizes:

- movement quality
- progressive overload
- exercise selection according to biomechanics
- fatigue management
- sufficient recovery
- high-quality working sets
- keeping heavy compound movements away from failure
- allowing smaller isolation movements to approach failure

Important:

This file describes Chengyi Tan's publicly presented training practice.

It does NOT mean every component is scientifically superior to all alternative training systems.

---

2. Split Structure

Push

Primary muscles:

- chest
- anterior / lateral deltoids
- triceps

Reported exercises:

1. Barbell bench press
2. Incline dumbbell press
3. Dips
4. Lying triceps extension
5. Y-raise / lateral raise

Training logic

The push session combines:

- one major pressing movement
- one additional chest press
- a bodyweight / compound pressing movement
- direct triceps work
- direct lateral-delt work

This creates a combination of:

- high mechanical loading
- muscle-specific work
- relatively efficient exercise selection

---

3. Pull

Primary muscles:

- latissimus dorsi
- upper back
- rear delts
- biceps

Reported exercises:

1. Single-arm cable pulldown
2. Neutral-grip pulldown
3. Single-arm supported row
4. Elbow-out / upper-back row
5. Cable curl

Training logic

The structure combines:

- vertical pulling
- unilateral pulling
- horizontal pulling
- upper-back work
- direct biceps work

The use of unilateral pulling allows individual sides to be trained separately.

This may be useful when an athlete has:

- asymmetry
- different strength between sides
- different scapular mechanics
- difficulty finding a symmetrical bilateral pulling pattern

However, unilateral work should not automatically be interpreted as superior to bilateral training.

---

4. Legs

Primary muscles:

- quadriceps
- hamstrings
- glutes
- spinal erectors
- hip stabilizers

Reported exercises:

1. Single-leg dumbbell Romanian deadlift
2. Bulgarian split squat
3. Squat / front squat variation
4. Barbell Romanian deadlift
5. Back extension

Training logic

The session combines:

- unilateral lower-body training
- squat pattern
- hip hinge
- posterior-chain work
- spinal-erector work

This creates a relatively posterior-chain-heavy lower-body structure.

5. Core Training Philosophy

Principle 1 — Quality before load

A heavier weight is not automatically better.

Progression should preserve:

- stable technique
- target-muscle loading
- appropriate range of motion
- controlled execution

If increasing load causes a major deterioration in technique, the increase may not represent useful progression.

---

6. Principle 2 — Compound movements and isolation movements should not be treated identically

Public explanations associated with this system emphasize different fatigue-management strategies for large compound lifts and smaller isolation exercises.

Compound movements

Examples:

- bench press
- squat
- Romanian deadlift

General approach:

- maintain technical margin
- avoid unnecessary failure
- use controlled RPE/RIR
- allow adequate recovery

Isolation movements

Examples:

- lateral raise
- curl
- cable work

These movements can generally be trained closer to failure because:

- systemic fatigue is usually lower
- technique breakdown is easier to control
- recovery cost is generally smaller

Important:

"Never train compounds to failure" should NOT be treated as an absolute scientific law.

The better Skill rule is:

«The closer an exercise is to technical failure, the more its fatigue cost and safety/technique consequences must be considered.»

---

7. Progressive Overload

The system places substantial emphasis on progression rather than randomly changing exercises.

Possible progression variables:

1. load
2. repetitions
3. execution quality
4. range of motion
5. proximity to failure
6. total productive volume

A practical progression hierarchy:

Stage 1

Improve technique at the same load.

Stage 2

Increase repetitions while maintaining technique.

Stage 3

Increase load while maintaining the target repetition range.

Stage 4

Continue progression until recovery or performance indicates that volume/intensity needs adjustment.

---

8. RIR / RPE Framework

A useful interpretation of the training philosophy:

Heavy compound movements

Typical target:

- approximately 1–3 RIR
- approximately RPE 7–9

The exact target should depend on:

- exercise
- training phase
- fatigue
- experience
- recovery

Isolation movements

Typical target:

- approximately 0–2 RIR
- sometimes technical failure

Again, this is a practical framework, not a rigid rule.

---

9. Rest and Recovery

The public training material emphasizes that PPL does NOT necessarily mean:

«3 training days + 1 rest day»

The cycle can be adjusted according to recovery.

Potential schedules:

High recovery

Push → Pull → Legs → Rest

Moderate recovery

Push → Pull → Rest → Legs → Push → Rest ...

Low recovery

Push → Rest → Pull → Rest → Legs → Rest

The important variable is not the name of the split.

The important variables are:

- weekly productive volume
- training quality
- performance trend
- muscle recovery
- joint recovery
- sleep
- life stress

10. Natural-Athlete Interpretation

For natural bodybuilding, the most useful lesson is NOT:

«"Everyone should train exactly like Chengyi Tan."»

Instead:

«Build a training system that provides sufficient stimulus while allowing the athlete to repeatedly perform high-quality sessions.»

This is particularly important for natural athletes because there is no reason to assume that elite-athlete training volume or frequency will automatically be recoverable by every trainee.

High priority

- individual exercise selection
- unilateral work when useful
- recovery-based scheduling

---

12. What Should NOT Be Copied Automatically

Do not automatically copy:

- exact exercise list
- exact number of sets
- exact repetitions
- exact weekly frequency
- exact rest schedule
- exact RPE
- exact progression speed
- exact training volume

理由：

谭成义的：

-遗传因素
- 训练年限 
-体型比例
-恢复能力
-生活方式
-比赛目标
-当前体能水平

与用户的不同。
